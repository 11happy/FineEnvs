## What the community built

Within a week of part 1, two people had built on it. Both are now in the
[FineEnvs catalog](https://github.com/adithya-s-k/FineEnvs/tree/main/07-simulation-environments), credited by name,
each with its own eval.

### PortSimEnv v2–v5, by Edgar Arakelyan (Scale AI)

Edgar moved PortSimEnv onto [AgentEnv](https://github.com/scaleapi/agentenv-framework) and made the week happen in
order. The agent no longer sees the whole week at once. A virtual clock moves it through watches. News arrives 12 to
24 hours ahead, the next six hours freeze, and the agent confirms berths as it goes. The week is graded once, at the
end, against what really happened. Each version builds on the one before:

- **v2, live:** news watch by watch, freezes and confirmations.
- **v3, marine:** the port's 7 pilots and 8 tugs, shared hour by hour with all of its real 2024 traffic, plus
  announced cuts.
- **v4, wind:** real wind from the port's anemometer (2023 to 2025) and the ECMWF forecasts as they were issued. The
  agent plans on the forecast and is graded on the wind that actually blew.
- **v5, lines:** every ship's shipping agent is its own agent. ETAs are often wrong. The planner can ask a ship for
  its ETA or agree an arrival hour, and ships berth first come, first served.

Twelve models played 15 weeks of each version.

- **Each version is harder.** Eight of the twelve models score lower at each step from live to marine to wind, and
  11 of 12 score lower on the lines weeks than on the same weeks without the ships' agents.
- **On v5, talking to the ships is what scores.** A re-planner that agrees arrival times with the ships' agents
  reaches 0.55. The same planner without asking reaches 0.24.
- **Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra lead** over all 60 weeks, at 0.857 and 0.851. Qwen3.8-27B, the one model small
  enough to fine-tune cheaply, finds a valid plan in 1 of 60.

The code is in [`07-simulation-environments/portsim-live`](https://github.com/adithya-s-k/FineEnvs/tree/main/07-simulation-environments/portsim-live),
every run is in the dataset [earakely-scale/PortSimEnv-AgentEnv](https://huggingface.co/datasets/earakely-scale/PortSimEnv-AgentEnv),
and the [Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/earakely-scale/PortSimEnv-AgentEnv) replays them on the same 3D quay.

### RailSimEnv, by Bhavish Pahwa

Bhavish took the recipe to rail. In [RailSimEnv](https://huggingface.co/spaces/bpHigh/RailSimEnv) the agent
dispatches Arriva's trains on the single-track Groningen–Leeuwarden line in the Netherlands after a real 2025
breakdown, collision, or signal or points failure. It holds, short-turns and cancels trains so that opposing trains
cross safely. Every plan is graded against a CP-SAT-proven optimum. It runs on OpenEnv with a 3D replay and is built
from Rijden de Treinen's open train archive and OpenStreetMap.

On 45 hard eval tasks, as reported by the author:

| Model | Reward | Legal plans | At the optimum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B | 0.556 | 39 of 45 | 12 |
| DeepSeek V4.1 Flash | 0.534 | 33 of 45 | 12 |
| Claude Sonnet 5.5 | 0.467 | 45 of 45 | 4 |
| GLM-5.3-Flash | 0.360 | 24 of 45 | 6 |

An open model leads here. The one closed model keeps every plan legal but rarely finds the best one. The code is in
[bp-high/RLSimEnvs](https://github.com/bp-high/RLSimEnvs).

### Add yours

The catalog takes environments whose code lives in FineEnvs and ones that stay in their authors' own repos. Every
entry has a manifest that credits its authors, a short card, and an eval card in a shared format. That shared format
is what lets this article show each environment's eval the same way, as in the chart above.
[Here is how to add one](https://github.com/adithya-s-k/FineEnvs/tree/main/07-simulation-environments#add-your-environment).
